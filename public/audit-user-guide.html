02

The big picture

An audit plan is the overall assurance plan for a period. A programme groups related audits. An audit is one scheduled inspection or review. A finding records what the auditor discovered against a question.

1. Publish a form→2. Plan & programme→3. Schedule→4. Record & confirm→5. Follow up
MethodHow it worksSample form
StandardRecord answers and classify findings. Confirm individual questions or complete the whole audit.Management system assurance
ScorableA Standard audit with numeric scores on applicable questions.5S & safety scored audit
ExpressA short walkabout or inspection. Save and send for review, or directly confirm when the operating process permits it.Workplace safety walkabout
Two different kinds of completion: confirming an audit finishes its inspection record. Linked incidents and actions can still be open and must be managed in the Incident module.

Subject areas are not company structure

Health & Safety, Environment and Quality describe what the plan covers. These are subject areas (sometimes called “domains” in older systems). They are separate from Business unit → Site → Location → Department, which describes where the work happens.

04

Your first practice run

Use the existing scored audit so you can learn the workflow before creating a plan. This example deliberately produces one incident, one corrective action and one preventive action.

About 15 minutesAUD-104 · Dublin · 5S & safety review4 questions · 3 follow-ups
1

Open the scored audit

Go to Audit → Audits, search AUD-104 and open Dublin · 5S & safety review. Use the Questions tab and set Question view to show all questions.

What you should see: The audit has four questions using the published 5S & safety scored audit form.
2

Confirm the first finding

For Are walkways clear and correctly marked?, answer No, choose Major Non-Conformance, score 1 and comment “Pedestrian segregation missing at the loading bay approach.” Tick Confirm and lock question 1, then choose Save findings.

Normal role: lead auditor. The sample employee auditee is Sam, so the form’s major-finding assignment rule routes the incident to him.

What you should see: Question 1 is locked and an Incident link appears. Progress is 25%; the saved score is provisionally 20% (1 out of 5).
3

Answer the remaining questions

Use these practice answers. The score must be within the question’s configured range.

QuestionAnswer / findingScoreComment
Equipment stored in designated locations?No · Minor Non-Conformance3Equipment storage areas are not marked.
Machine guards in place and inspected?N/A findingLeave blankNo machinery is included in this inspection scope.
Emergency equipment accessible?Yes · Opportunity for Improvement4Access is clear; improve extinguisher visibility.

For N/A, choose the N/A finding type; an answer and score are not required, but its comment is.

What you should see: All four questions are ready for confirmation. The N/A question will be excluded from the score denominator.
4

Complete the audit record

Enter a Closing note, for example “Inspection complete; route segregation, storage marking and equipment visibility follow-up assigned.” Choose Complete & confirm audit.

What you should see: The audit is Completed, questions are locked and progress is 100%. The overall score rounds to 53%: (1 + 3 + 4) ÷ (5 + 5 + 5).
5

Follow the work into Incident

Read the generated links: a major finding creates an incident, a minor finding a corrective action, and the improvement opportunity a preventive action. Open one link, inspect its source, then use Open source audit to return.

What you should see: The audit is completed while its follow-up work is still open. Continue those workflows using the Incident guide.
Practising again: confirmed questions cannot be edited in this prototype. Schedule another audit, or reload to restore the sample records when you are ready to discard all current demo changes.

06

Build and reuse audit templates

Suggested people: Sofia designs; Alex publishes. Audit → Templates previews the available published forms; Configuration → Form Builder is where they are created.

1

Choose the method first

In Form Builder → Create form, enter a name, choose Audit, then choose Standard, Scorable or Express. Review the standard title, site, scheduled date, lead auditor and scope fields.

What you should see: The method determines how its questions will be answered and, for Scorable, scored.
2

Design questions people can answer

Add section headings to group questions. Drag or add components such as Yes / No, dropdown or long text. Enter labels, guidance, required settings and options. Use the Components search to reuse a field and then reorder it as needed.

Example: section “Emergency readiness”, Yes / No question “Are emergency exits clear?”, help text explaining what evidence to check.

What you should see: The right-hand preview reflects the form. Custom answer fields become the audit’s questions; section headings organise them.
3

Set scoring and visibility where relevant

For a scored form, configure each question’s minimum, maximum and increment, such as 0 to 5 in steps of 1. Review question / template site visibility, default finding suggestions and the major-finding assignee option.

What you should see: Only questions visible for the scheduled site are included. A suggested finding is not a completed answer.
4

Publish for scheduling

Review the form and publish it. Open Audit → Templates to preview the published version, then use it as a programme default or choose it when scheduling an audit.

What you should see: Draft forms do not appear as usable scheduling templates. An audit keeps a snapshot of its chosen published version.
Extend one audit without changing every future audit. Use Add question to this audit before completion. To reuse the entire question set, choose Copy to builder draft, review the new draft in Form Builder and publish it for future scheduling.

The mobile option describes intended availability for assigned users; this prototype uses the responsive web app and does not connect a separate mobile app.

07

Set up plans and programmes

Suggested people: Alex creates the plan; Priya or another programme owner coordinates its audits.

1

Create the assurance plan

Open Audit → Audit plans → Create plan. Enter the plan name, start and end years, relevant business areas and the remaining descriptive information. Save.

Example: “Northstar annual assurance plan”, covering the current year, Health & Safety, Environment and Quality.

What you should see: The plan is the parent container for its programmes.
2

Create a programme inside it

Open the plan and choose Create programme. Enter its name, programme type, owner, review date and description. Choose a compatible published default form.

Example: “5S & workplace safety”, Standard programme type with a Scorable audit form.

What you should see: The programme appears in the plan and can now hold scheduled audits and recurring rules.
3

Monitor and maintain the programme

Open the programme to schedule audits, add a recurring schedule or inspect its audit list. Keep the owner and review date up to date.

What you should see: The programme shows the work it groups. Completion is available only when its audit and recurrence conditions are satisfied.
Date and archive rules: the plan’s start year locks after a programme exists. Its end year cannot exclude existing audits or review dates. Completed audits / programmes and records linked to incidents or actions cannot be archived. To archive eligible parents, first archive eligible children and pause their schedules. Archive preserves data; it is not deletion.
08

Schedule an audit

Suggested person: Sam, a lead auditor with scheduling permission in the relevant scope. In the demo, use Alex.

1

Choose the programme and template

On Audit → Audits choose Schedule audit, or schedule directly from a programme. Enter the name, choose an active programme, the department / site and a published compatible Audit form.

What you should see: The selected form must contain questions visible for that site. Programme options include their plan year to distinguish annual versions.
2

Assign people and time

Choose the lead auditor, Employee or Third-party auditee and the auditee. Set the start date / time and duration with its unit. Add secondary auditors, people observed or other participants when useful.

For a supplier audit, select Jamie Walsh at Greenline Engineering. Alex maintains this record in Settings → Third-party auditees.

What you should see: The audit records who is auditing, who is being audited and when the work is planned. An external auditee is a contact, not an automatically created app user.
3

Explain the scope and save

Describe the scope / objectives and any additional information. The initial audit date must fall within the plan period. Save the audit.

What you should see: A new audit appears in the register with unanswered questions from the template snapshot.
4

Prepare for fieldwork

Open the audit and use Hard copy for a blank fieldwork report. Use Details & evidence for notes and evidence names or reference links; click Save notes & evidence.

What you should see: The hard-copy preview can be downloaded as HTML or printed / saved as PDF. Notes and references stay with the record; this demo does not upload file contents.

Edit schedule details can adjust permitted information before completion. The programme, department / site and template are fixed on an existing audit to preserve its recorded answers.

09

Record, score and confirm findings

Suggested person: Sam, the lead auditor. Work through the Questions tab, one question at a time or with the all-questions view.

  1. Read the question’s guidance and gather evidence.
  2. Enter the answer, select its finding type and record the finding comment.
  3. For Scorable questions, enter a valid score within its minimum / maximum / increment.
  4. Use Save findings to preserve work. For a Standard / Scorable question ready to finish, tick Confirm and lock question before saving.
  5. When every question is ready, enter the closing note and choose Complete & confirm audit.
Confirmation routes follow-up. Saving an unconfirmed finding preserves progress. Confirming and locking a question creates the relevant linked incident or action once. Completing the audit confirms all remaining questions.

What each finding creates

Finding typeFollow-up outcome
Major Non-ConformanceCreates an Audit Non-Conformance incident in the Incident module. It may be Draft if required custom incident answers still need completing.
Minor Non-ConformanceCreates a corrective action in Incident → Actions.
Opportunity for ImprovementCreates a preventive action in Incident → Actions.
Insufficient FindingCreates an audit trail item to revisit in a later audit.
Conforming / Observation / Good PracticeRecords the finding without automatic incident or action creation.
N/AExcludes the question from scoring. Requires a comment explaining why it is not applicable.

Non-conformances, improvement opportunities and insufficient findings require a comment. Use objective evidence, not just “failed”. A confirmed question is locked; this prototype does not provide an audit reopening workflow.

Understand the score

Overall score = total saved applicable scores ÷ total applicable maximum scores × 100

With scores 1, 3 and 4 out of 5, plus one N/A question: 8 ÷ 15 = 53% when rounded. N/A contributes neither score nor maximum. Before all applicable questions are scored, the displayed score is provisional; it reflects only saved scored answers, not a final result.

Follow the source, not duplicate records

Use the generated Incident / Action links. Assignment of a major incident follows the form’s rule: it can use the employee auditee, otherwise the lead auditor; third-party auditees fall back to the lead. If the generated incident is Draft, complete its required report answers and submit it in Incident.

Use Open source audit to return. Manage tasks, completion and effectiveness checks in the Incident guide. The audit can be completed while those records remain open.

Perform and review an Express audit

Suggested people: Sam records the walkabout; Liam reviews it. Demonstrate both parts as Alex.

1

Open the sample walkabout

Open AUD-105 · Cork · weekly walkabout. Answer its four questions, including the observation text. Select the appropriate finding types and comments.

What you should see: The form is the published Workplace safety walkabout Express template.
2

Send it for review

Enter the Closing note, choose a reviewer and click Save & send for review. All required questions must be ready.

What you should see: The audit becomes Awaiting review. Answers are read-only, but the reviewer can check and adjust the finding classifications and comments.
3

Confirm the review

As the reviewer, check the evidence, classifications and comments, enter the required review comments and click Confirm review & findings.

What you should see: The audit becomes Reviewed, findings lock and any incident / action follow-up is generated.
100% answered is not formally completed. An Express audit can have all answers saved and still await submission or confirmation. Where the operating process permits it, Complete & confirm audit offers direct confirmation instead of the separate review route.
11

Carry unresolved evidence forward

An Insufficient Finding means there was not enough evidence to reach a conclusion. The audit can finish, while its trail item remains for a later audit in the same programme lineage.

1

Find what needs another check

Open Audit → Findings & audit trail → Audit trail. Filter Open items and inspect the source.

The sample AUD-101 · September management review asks to recheck contractor competency evidence.

What you should see: You can see the source audit, original question and the evidence that must be revisited.
2

Carry it into the next audit

Open AUD-103 · Management controls · Dublin. In its extension area find Evidence to revisit and choose Carry into this audit for the competency item.

What you should see: A follow-up question is added, retaining the original comment and source audit reference. The same item is not added twice to this audit.
3

Resolve it with a conclusion

Gather the missing evidence. Answer the carried question, write a clear comment, choose a conclusive finding such as Conforming and confirm / lock it, then save.

Example: “Current competency certificates inspected for all contractors in this audit scope.”

What you should see: The original trail item is marked resolved with a link to this audit. Insufficient Finding or N/A would leave it unresolved.

Use Open / Resolved / All, programme, site and text filters to find trail items. An unresolved trail is an outstanding evidence check, not automatically an Incident task.

12

Set up recurring audits

Suggested person: Alex or a programme owner with schedule management permission. A recurring schedule is a rule that generates individual audits; pausing it does not erase audits already generated.

1

Create the schedule

Open Audit → Recurring schedules → Create schedule, or use Copy as recurring schedule on an existing audit. Set the programme, template, people, department, duration and scope.

Choose Daily, Weekly, Monthly, Quarterly or Annually, enter the initial date / time and notification lead days, and optionally set an end date.

What you should see: The saved rule has a clear starting point and can be paused or resumed.
2

Preview before generating

Open the rule and select a preview count of 3, 6 or 12. Review the dates, planned notification dates, plan / programme and any already existing audit markers.

Try RS-001 · Cork · weekly walkabout. The first occurrence already exists as AUD-105.

What you should see: The preview shows the first requested number of occurrences from the rule’s initial anchor, including any that already exist.
3

Generate the missing audits

Click Generate previewed audits. For the sample weekly rule, a six-occurrence preview creates five new audits because the first already exists. Repeating generation of those same occurrences does not duplicate them.

What you should see: The generated audits appear in the register. Annual plan / programme rollover is created or reused when occurrences extend beyond the original plan period.
This prototype needs the Generate button. Lead days demonstrate when notifications would be due; no email or cron service runs here. Monthly dates keep their original anchor: a 31st falls on the last day of a shorter month, then returns to the 31st when possible.

Use Pause schedule to prevent generation. Review and edit the rule before resuming. Dates are illustrative tenant-local date / time values in this demo.

14

Questions and practice checklist

Why can’t I find my template when scheduling?

Check that the form is Published, belongs to Audit, is compatible with the programme method and has questions visible for the selected site. A Draft form cannot be used.

Why can’t I finish the audit?

Check required answers, finding comments and valid numeric scores. N/A needs a reason but does not need an answer or score. Enter the closing note. An Express submission also needs a reviewer; review confirmation needs review comments.

Why can’t I change a question I have confirmed?

Confirmation locks the question and routes follow-up. Completed audits are also locked. This prototype has no audit reopening workflow. For practice, create another audit or deliberately reload to reset demo data.

Why is the audit completed while its incident is still open?

Audit completion means the inspection and findings are recorded. Incident investigation, actions, tasks and effectiveness checks are separate follow-up work. Use the linked record and the Incident guide.

Why did a major finding create a Draft incident?

The Incident report template may contain required custom questions that cannot be answered from the audit alone. Complete those answers and submit the report in Incident. Do not create a second incident for the same finding.

Why did recurring generation add fewer audits than the preview count?

Already generated occurrences are not duplicated. The preview includes existing dates and starts at the schedule’s original initial date, rather than automatically moving forward from today.

Why can’t I archive this record?

Completed audits / programmes and records with linked incidents or actions are protected. Eligible parent records also require eligible children to be archived and their schedules paused first. Read the displayed blocker before proceeding.

What do auditee, non-conformance and audit trail mean?

Auditee: the employee or third party whose work is being reviewed. Non-conformance: a finding that the requirement being checked has not been met. Audit trail here: an unresolved evidence question carried forward to a later audit. History: the record of changes and decisions on one audit.
