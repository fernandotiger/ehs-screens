02

The big picture

An incident is the event. An investigation explains why it happened. An action fixes or prevents a problem. A task is one piece of the work needed to finish that action.

1. Report→2. Investigate→3. Act→4. Verify effectiveness→5. Close

Corrective action

Fix a problem that has happened. Example: install signs after an inspection found an unmarked pedestrian route.

Preventive action

Reduce the chance of a future problem. Example: refresh loading-bay training after several similar near misses.

Finished work is not the same as a solved problem. An action goes from Open / In progress → Awaiting effectiveness → Effective or Ineffective. An incident can close only when its required investigation and follow-up conditions are satisfied.

How the company structure fits

Business unit → Site → Location → Department

Example: Operations → Cork Distribution → Warehouse → Logistics. Choosing the department identifies its parent location and site. Assigned people and suggested roles should operate within this structure.

Audit findings can start this workflow too

A confirmed major audit non-conformance creates an incident. A minor non-conformance creates a corrective action; an opportunity for improvement creates a preventive action. Use Open source audit on an originating record to return to the audit.

06

Prepare forms and settings

Suggested people: Sofia drafts the form; Alex reviews, publishes and selects the type rules. Forms come from the shared Form Builder.

1

Create the right kind of form

Open Configuration → Form Builder → Create form. Enter a name, choose the Incident module, then choose Report or Investigation as the purpose. Keep the system fields and add the custom questions you need.

What you should see: A report form captures the event; an investigation form captures the follow-up. They are separate template purposes.
2

Add or reuse questions

Drag a component into the form, or use its add control. Configure its label, help text, required setting and options. Use the Components search to reuse an existing field; selecting it adds a copy at the end. Reorder questions to match the way people work.

Examples: report question “Was the area made safe?”; investigation question “How was the root cause verified?”. Check any site-specific visibility rules.

What you should see: The form preview shows your questions in the intended order. Required questions must be answered when submitting or completing the relevant workflow.
3

Publish and connect the forms

Publish the finished form. Open Incident → Settings → Incident types, select a type and choose its published report and investigation templates. Review subtypes, assignment rules, investigation requirements, confidentiality, evidence and investigation target days. Save the type settings.

What you should see: Published compatible forms can be selected for new work. Draft forms cannot.
Existing records keep their template snapshot. Publishing a revised form changes future records; it does not rewrite the questions or answers on incidents already created.

The other Settings tabs

TabWhat Alex maintains

ReporteesInternal or external destinations used to describe who should be informed. The demo does not send reports or notifications.

Type names are predefined in this prototype. Activate or deactivate the relevant subtypes and choices; historical values remain readable.

07

Record an incident

Suggested person: Jordan, the reporter. Record the facts available now; the investigation supplies the later analysis.

1

Start the report

On Incident → Incidents, create a new incident. Choose the published report template and the type / subtype, then enter a clear title, severity and the event date and time.

Example title: “Near miss between forklift and pedestrian at loading bay”. Use the time the event happened, rather than the time you entered the record.

What you should see: The template’s custom questions appear alongside the standard incident fields.
2

Describe where and who

Choose Department, describe the precise location and shift, and assign an investigator. Use Add person for each witness or injured person; select Employee / Third party and their involvement. Add injury information where relevant.

Example: Logistics at Cork, loading bay 2, Sam as investigator, Jordan as witness.

What you should see: The site follows the selected department. Investigator requirements follow the incident type’s single or multiple assignment rule.
3

Describe the event and immediate response

Enter what happened and what was done immediately. Complete required custom questions and record appropriate evidence names or links. Review confidentiality and reportee information where shown.

Example immediate response: “Vehicle stopped and loading bay isolated while the route was checked.”

What you should see: The report separates the event description from later root-cause findings.
4

Save a draft or submit

Choose Save draft if you still need information. A named draft can be incomplete. Choose Submit incident when the required report fields are complete.

What you should see: A draft remains Draft. A submitted report enters the active workflow; validation explains any missing required information.
Confidentiality in this presentation: the flag demonstrates how a sensitive report could be marked. It does not restrict Alex’s access or implement production privacy controls.
08

Investigate the causes

Suggested person: Sam, the assigned investigator. Use the incident record’s Investigation tab or the Investigations register.

1

Open the investigation

Choose Start investigation or Open investigation. Select the published investigation form and complete its required questions.

What you should see: The investigation is linked to this incident and keeps a snapshot of its selected template.
2

Record findings and causes

Explain the facts, investigation findings and root cause. Select relevant causes from the grouped list. Record applicable safety hazards or environmental aspects and impacts. Add evidence references and identify whether further action is required.

Example: “The pedestrian route was removed during a layout change and the change review did not include traffic segregation.”

What you should see: The record explains why the event happened and what follow-up is needed.
3

Record absence or impact if relevant

Use Add absence for lost-time or restricted-duty periods. Enter the start and end dates and the correct kind. Record environmental duration, unit or impact details only where relevant.

What you should see: Reports can distinguish lost-time absence from restricted duties. Lost days are inclusive calendar days, not working days.
4

Save or finish

Use Save progress while collecting evidence. When the required findings, causes, custom questions and closing information are complete, choose Complete investigation. If action is required, raise or link it from the incident’s Actions tab.

What you should see: The investigation becomes Completed. Completing it does not automatically complete actions or close the incident.

A reopened investigation returns the incident to an unfinished investigation state. The incident cannot close until its required investigation is complete again.

09

Create and complete the work

Suggested people: Sam defines the work; Priya or another assigned owner carries it out. An action can contain several tasks, or no tasks if its work can be recorded directly.

1

Raise or link an action

On an incident’s Actions tab choose Raise action. Enter a clear description, Corrective / Preventive kind, department, owner, due date, priority and category. Choose Save action.

If an existing action addresses this incident too, select it and use Link action instead of making duplicate work.

What you should see: The action appears against the incident. A shared action can be associated with more than one incident.
2

Break the work into tasks if useful

Open the action and choose Add task. Enter the task title, owner, department, due date and priority; choose Save task.

For “Refresh loading bay markings”, a task might be “Agree pedestrian route with logistics team”.

What you should see: The task belongs to its parent action and is also visible in the Tasks register.
3

Complete each task

Open a task, record any evidence references and enter a completion comment explaining what actually happened. Choose Complete task.

What you should see: The task shows completed. The parent action’s completed-task count increases.
4

Complete the action and plan the check

After all of its tasks are finished, enter the action’s Completion comment, Effectiveness reviewer and Effectiveness review date. Choose Complete action.

For a same-day demo, choose today as the review date. For a real workflow, allow enough time to observe whether the change works.

What you should see: The action becomes Awaiting effectiveness. It is not yet verified as Effective.
Audit-created actions: a corrective action raised by an audit can exist without a separate incident. It keeps its audit source link. Preventive actions can also exist independently, for example following a trend review.
10

Check effectiveness and close

Suggested people: Liam checks the outcome; an authorised incident manager closes the incident. Separate the reviewer from the action owner in your proposed operating model, while demonstrating both as Alex.

1

Make the effectiveness decision

Open an action marked Awaiting effectiveness. On or after the planned review date, choose Effective or Ineffective, enter a Review evidence / comment and click Record effectiveness.

Use observable evidence: “Three shift checks found no blocked pedestrian routes.” A completion comment such as “done” is not an effectiveness check.

What you should see: The action retains the reviewer, review date and outcome comment.
2

Follow up if the action did not work

If it is Ineffective, choose Create follow-up action. Describe the revised approach, assign it and complete its work and effectiveness review.

What you should see: The original result stays in history. The follow-up preserves the source incident associations and any audit provenance.
3

Check the closure conditions

Return to the incident. Its required investigation must be complete. If action is required, it must have a linked action. All linked work must meet the effectiveness conditions, including effective follow-ups for ineffective actions.

What you should see: The closure panel either allows closure or explains the remaining blockers.
4

Record the decision to close

Enter a meaningful Closure note and choose Close incident. Read the History tab to check the sequence of decisions.

What you should see: The incident is Closed. Reopen incident can return it to the active workflow if new information requires further work.
Shared action reminder: ACT-093 is linked to INC-261 and INC-245. Review its associated incidents before making changes, because the outcome contributes to closure checks on each.
11

Turn patterns into prevention

Suggested people: Ethan spots the pattern; Sam or an EHS manager creates preventive work.

1

Preview a meaningful group

Open Incident → Trends & prevention. Set the event date range and any type, subtype, department or cause criteria. Preview the trend and read the matching incidents before deciding on an action.

For the samples, include September 2026 and Near Miss events at Cork.

What you should see: You see which events match, rather than relying on a total without the underlying records.
2

Create prevention from the matches

Create a preventive action, explaining the common issue and desired improvement. Set its owner and due date, then save.

What you should see: The action stores the criteria and a snapshot of matched incident IDs. Initial associations include matching open incidents.
3

Keep the evidence understandable

On the saved action use Refresh trend snapshot if you want to capture the latest matches. Review the associated incidents separately.

What you should see: The saved trend results update. Refreshing the snapshot does not automatically rewrite the action’s incident associations.

The preventive work then follows the same task completion and effectiveness checks as any other action.

  1. Choose the report and date range; narrow the site, department or other available filters.
  2. Read the displayed rows and counts.
  3. Use Export CSV for spreadsheet work. Alex can configure columns in Settings → Export layout.
  4. Use Print / save PDF and choose “Save as PDF” in the browser’s print dialog.

Exported custom fields come from the saved report template data. A missing field on an older record may legitimately be blank.

13

Questions and practice checklist

Why can’t I submit an incident?

Check the required report fields, the type / subtype, assigned investigator rules and required custom questions. Use Save draft while information is incomplete.

Why is Complete action disabled?

Finish every task linked to that action first. Then provide a completion comment, effectiveness reviewer and review date.

Why can’t I record effectiveness yet?

The planned review date may be in the future. The check becomes available on that date. Use today’s date when deliberately practising the whole workflow in one session.

Why can’t I close the incident?

Read the closure blockers. Common reasons are an unfinished required investigation, no action when action is required, unfinished linked work, or an ineffective action whose follow-up is not yet effective. Enter a closure note too.

Where has my template gone?

Check that it is Published, its module is Incident, and its purpose matches Report or Investigation. Check the incident type’s template settings and any site visibility rules.

Will the system send an email or store my uploaded evidence?

This prototype demonstrates the fields and workflow. It does not send emails or external reports, run real background notifications, or upload evidence files to a server. Evidence names and reference links are retained only in the demo record.

What do reportee, root cause and effectiveness mean?

Reportee: a person or destination that should receive information. Root cause: the underlying reason the event occurred. Effectiveness: evidence that the completed action achieved its intended result. Closure: the final recorded decision after the required checks.
